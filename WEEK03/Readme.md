Sample Superstore Sales & Profit Analysis

Project Overview

This project analyzes the Sample Superstore dataset using Microsoft Power BI. The objective is to evaluate business performance and identify patterns in sales, profit, product performance, discounting, seasonality, and geographic profitability.

The analysis covers the period from **2014 to 2017** and was completed as part of the AnalystLab Africa data analytics project.

Objectives

The project focuses on:

- Analyzing overall sales and profit performance
- Identifying sales and profitability trends over time
- Evaluating product and subcategory performance
- Comparing discount levels with profitability
- Identifying high-performing and low-performing states
- Investigating recurring loss-making products and categories
- Providing data-driven business recommendations

Tools Used

- Microsoft Power BI
- DAX
- Microsoft Excel / CSV dataset

Key Metrics

The dashboard includes the following measures:

- Total Sales
- Total Profit
- Total Orders
- Total Customers
- Total Quantity
- Average Sales
- Profit Margin
- Average Discount

Key Insights

- Sales show strong growth across the 2014–2017 period.
- November, December, and September recorded the highest overall sales.
- Copiers, Phones, and Accessories were among the most profitable subcategories.
- Tables, Bookcases, and Supplies showed the weakest profitability performance.
- High discounts do not always result in losses. For example, Binders remained profitable despite a high average discount.
- California and New York were the strongest states by profit.
- Some low-volume subcategories, including Labels and Envelopes, showed strong profit margins.

Recommendations

- Review pricing and profitability for recurring loss-making subcategories.
- Monitor discounts at product and subcategory level rather than using a single discount strategy.
- Protect and scale high-performing products and profitable markets.
- Improve planning around the high-demand September–December period.
- Investigate low-profit products individually before making product rationalization decisions.
